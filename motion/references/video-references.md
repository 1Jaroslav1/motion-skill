# Video: factual analysis and combining references

If a file is provided, work with its real frames and available audio. If a URL is given, actually open the page first and obtain the available material. Don't describe editing, motion, frames or music from a title/link. A page may give only text or a poster: state the extent of access explicitly. If the video is unavailable, name the unavailable material; independent staging can continue without attributing observations from that reference to it.

Build a map of scenes and transitions with timecodes: boundaries, durations/shot sizes, composition and layers, hero action, typography, camera path and speed, material/light, seam carrier, sound and rhythm. For important seams extract a dense frame sequence before/inside/after and watch the motion at speed. Don't determine exact speed, easing or sound quality from a single still. Three picked frames are not a frame-by-frame analysis of the whole video.

Separate observation from transfer: **seen/heard in a specific interval → useful principle → own solution for the current story**. Keep the source and access status. Analysing someone else's technique doesn't grant the right to automatically include their video, model, font or music in the output.

## If three references must be combined

Use all three meaningfully. Record which characteristic you take from each: for example, spatial composition from the first, typography from the second, camera/rhythm from the third. This is an example distribution, not a mandatory assignment. Note compatible and conflicting properties and create one shared system, not three independent fragments.

Adapt palette, logo, materials, hero and environment as requested; they don't have to copy the sources. Keep one causal thread and scale of motion. Briefly show a map "reference → characteristic → application", then build your own storyboard. Combining references is subordinate to the three core mechanics.
