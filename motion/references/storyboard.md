# Dynamic storyboard

Needed before building the full video. It turns a simple idea into executable staging and preserves the three core mechanics.

1. Extract from the brief: goal, audience, key message, mandatory text, format, duration, sound, allowed resources and output files. Don't invent unknown real facts; make reversible creative decisions yourself.
2. State one thread: what acts, what changes, what idea the result proves. It can be a change of material, a transfer of motion, a causal chain, an unfolding space or another original approach. Don't copy a previous video's sequence under a new name.
3. For each scene give 3–5 short options that differ in action, composition/space, metaphor or transition. Changing colour and font alone is not a new option. Pick the strongest for the overall development; show the outcome of the choice and a short practical reason without revealing internal reasoning.
4. Fill in the storyboard for all scenes and seams, then check perception time and risks. It is a working plan, not a mandatory queue of user approvals. If intermediate approval is explicitly requested, respect that boundary.

## Shot card

| Field | What to fix |
|---|---|
| Time and meaning | Interval, main accent, role in the overall story |
| States and action | What was there, what triggers the change, what the hero does, reaction, result |
| Space | Composition, fore/mid/background, light, material |
| Text | Exact phrase, action of letters/words, reading window |
| Camera and optics | Path, target, rotation/speed, shot size, focus and blur |
| Links | Entry from previous shot, transition carrier, exit into the next |
| Sound | Musical bar, SFX and synced event, or explicit silent |
| Check | Main risk, actual verification method, acceptable technical fallback |

Design each transition together with both scenes. For a match cut, align the projected silhouette, scale, orientation and direction of motion. For a mask, the next scene must genuinely appear inside it; a random overlay after a finished cut doesn't prove such a transition. In a single location keep its poses, perspective, light and speeds; tie an object reveal to real occlusion/action.

Before the full build, test one representative risk at real quality and in motion, with sound if there is any. Choose the probe length by perception and the brief; don't cram all events into a mandatory 5–7 seconds. The probe replaces neither the whole storyboard nor the iteration after the full build.
